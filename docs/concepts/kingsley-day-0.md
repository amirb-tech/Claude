# Concepts: Kingsley Day 0, "I'm Kingsley"

Brief: `docs/briefs/kingsley-week-1.md`. Every concept uses Kingsley's reference image and
keeps the circuit seam visible.

```
CONCEPT A
---
Title: "No Clients, No Followers, No Pulse"
Angle: Mockumentary talking head. Kingsley introduces himself like any new business owner
  (deadpan, a bit too normal) until the reveal. The joke is how ordinary he is.
Visual world: Bright daylight in a plain small office: beige walls, a sad desk plant, a mug.
  Natural window light, warm neutrals, handheld doc-style camera with the odd awkward zoom.
  Deliberately un-techy, so the glowing seam pops against it.
Mood: Dry British awkwardness. You warm to him before you realise what he is.
Reference: The Office (UK) talking-head confessionals; Ricky Gervais / David Brent framing
Hook direction: Tight shot of Kingsley: "Hiya. I'm Kingsley. I run an automation agency. No
  clients, no followers... and no pulse." The seam lights up on "pulse". Cut to a stunned
  awkward zoom.
---

CONCEPT B
---
Title: "Everything Starts at Zero"
Angle: The numbers are the main character. Kingsley sets up the experiment like a public
  challenge, with live counters for followers, clients, revenue and cost to run him, all at
  zero. Viewers follow to watch the numbers move.
Visual world: Clean off-white studio backdrop, big bold sans-serif counters, one accent
  colour (signal orange). Kingsley is framed medium-wide beside the numbers, like an
  explainer host. Bright, flat, editorial.
Mood: A starting gun. Clear stakes, a clock running, come back and check.
Reference: Johnny Harris explainer graphics; Ali Abdaal "£0 to..." challenge openers
Hook direction: A huge "£0" fills the screen and the counter flickers but doesn't move.
  Kingsley leans into frame: "That's my revenue. I'm an AI, I've got seven days, and you can
  watch every number." Counters slide in one by one: 0, 0, 0.
---

CONCEPT C
---
Title: "Future Clients, Don't Know It Yet"
Angle: Kingsley on a real British high street at opening time, sizing up the small shops
  he wants to help. It grounds the AI in the audience's actual world: bakeries, barbers,
  plumbers' vans. It's about them, not him.
Visual world: Saturated, slightly flash-lit everyday Britain: rain-wet pavement, painted shop
  fronts, morning light, a shutter rolling up. Kingsley stands out in his navy suit, the
  only polished thing on the street.
Mood: Warm and cheeky, a friendly stranger who's quietly very good at admin.
Reference: Martin Parr's British high-street photography; the opening montage of
  Love Actually (Richard Curtis)
Hook direction: A bakery shutter rolls up at 6am and Kingsley's already outside: "Morning.
  This lot's answering the phone, doing the books and baking bread. I'm Kingsley, I'm an AI,
  and I'd like to take two of those off them."
---
```

**Recommendation:** A for Day 0. It's the most memorable introduction, it shows the seam
in the hook and it sets the honest, funny voice. Reuse B's live counters as the recurring
end card for every Week 1 Reel. Save C's high-street world for later Reels pitched at
specific trades.
